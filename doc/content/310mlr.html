Question 3 Does it seem that mileage is a strong predictor of price?

Mileage does not seem to be a strong predictor of price from the linear model. Although the p-value is very low, the variability is high as shown by the residual standard error, while the low R² shows that much of the variability in Price cannot be explained by Mileage. Furthermore, the scatterplot shows a high level of variance across data points and no clear linear relationship.

Question 4 Why is mileage not a perfect predictor of price?

Mileage is not a perfect predictor of price because many other variables influence the retail price of the car, and mileage by itself is likely not a strong predictor of price. Other variables would include the original sale price of the car, the model and year, the size and features, and the physical condition, which would all factor into the final retail price.

Question 7 In this case, the variable with the largest estimated coefficient does have the smallest p-value, but that is not necessarily true in general. Explain why not.

Variables with a greater range in the data will have smaller coefficients compared to variables with smaller ranges since small changes in value won’t influence the model as much. Therefore, a variable with a huge distribution of numbers such as mileage could have a low coefficient and low p-value, while another variable on a smaller scale of values could have a high coefficient and high p-value.
