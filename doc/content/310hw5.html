Exercise 5.16

Suppose a researcher wants to learn about a possible relationship between the main ingredient in dog food (salmon, chicken, beef) and the amount of energy that dogs have. The researcher obtains 45 Border Collies and randomly assigns each dog to one of the three diets. She then measures the average amount of time each dog sleeps per 24 hours for the next week as an indication of the amount of energy each dog has.

A. Identify the explanatory and response variables in this study.

Explanatory variable: type of dog food

Response variable: sleep time

B. Is this an observational study or a randomized experiment? Explain how you know.

This is a randomized experiment because the dogs are being randomly assigned to one of the three diets, and the explanatory variable is being manipulated by the researcher.

C. Even though the subjects in this study were not randomly selected from a population, it’s still appropriate to conduct an analysis of variance. Explain why.

The subjects were not randomly selected but were randomly assigned to one of the three conditions, making inferences of differences between groups appropriate.

Exercise 5.38

An experiment was conducted to compare three different methods of repairing a meniscus (cartilage in the knee). Eighteen lightly embalmed cadaveric specimens were used, with each being randomly assigned to one of the three treatments: vertical suture, meniscus arrow, FasT-Fix. Each knee was evaluated on three different response variables: load at failure, stiffness, and displacement. Here we will concentrate on the stiffness response variable.

library(Stat2Data)
data("Meniscus")

A. Give the hypotheses that would be tested in an ANOVA procedure for this dataset.

\(\H_{0}\): \(\mu_{1}\) = \(\mu_{2}\) = \(\mu_{3}\)

\(\H_{A}\): At least one \(\mu_{i}\) is not equal

B. Show that the conditions for ANOVA are met for these data.

c1 <- aov(Stiffness~factor(Method), Meniscus)
plot(c1, 1:2)

plot(TukeyHSD(c1))

The residuals vs fitted values plot roughly displays constant variance and a near zero mean inference line, the Q-Q plot does adhere to the theoretical normal line, and the Tukey plot displays significant differences in the mean levels of the groups.

C. Conduct an ANOVA. Report the ANOVA table and interpret the results. Do the data provide strong evidence that the mean value of stiffness differs based on the type of meniscus repair? Explain.

anova(c1)

Exercise 5.58

A researcher wanted to know if the mean salaries of men and women are different. She chose a stratified random sample of 280 people from the 2000 U.S. Census consisting of men and women from New York State, Oregon, Arizona, and Iowa.

A. Is a person’s sex significant in predicting their salary? Explain your conclusions.

Yes, a person’s sex is significant in predicting their salary since the p-value is 0.000, indicating a significant difference in salary between men and women. However, other factors likely also play a significant role in determining salary.

B. What R^2 value does the ANOVA model have? Is this good? Explain.

The R^2 value of 4.29 is not great and shows that the percentage of variability in salary that can be explained by sex in this model is very small, suggesting that other variables should be included in the model.

C. The researcher did not look at residual plots. What conclusions do you reach about the ANOVA after examining these plots? Explain.

From the heavy-tailed distribution of the Q-Q plot and nonconstant spread of the residuals, we can conclude that an ANOVA model may not be the best predictive fit for this particular data.
